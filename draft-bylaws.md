# Kalamazoo Hollow Square draft bylaws

Draft for board review, November 18, 2026. Proposed choices include one-year terms, a calendar fiscal year, and at least two board meetings each year. Effective only upon adoption after incorporation. Officer names belong in the election minutes rather than in these continuing rules.

## Article 1 Name and purposes

The corporation is Kalamazoo Hollow Square, a Michigan nonprofit corporation organized on a nonstock, directorship basis. Its purposes and activities shall conform to its articles of incorporation and Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

## Article 2 Participation

The corporation has no members with statutory voting rights. Singers, volunteers, donors, and program participants do not become voting members through participation or contributions. The board shall welcome participant suggestions and provide opportunities for community input on programs.

## Article 3 Board of directors

The board manages the corporation's affairs and has five directors. It may change that number by amending these bylaws, but never below three. Each director has one vote, regardless of the number of offices held.

The incorporator selects the initial directors by written instrument. Initial directors serve until the first annual board meeting and until their successors are elected and qualified. At each annual meeting, the board elects directors for one-year terms and until their successors are elected and qualified. Directors may be reelected without a term limit.

A director may resign by written notice to the corporation. The remaining directors may fill a vacancy by affirmative vote of a majority of the directors remaining in office, even if fewer than a quorum, to the extent permitted by Michigan law. A replacement serves the remainder of the unexpired term and until a successor is elected and qualified.

A director may be removed for cause by affirmative vote of a majority of all directors then in office, consistent with Michigan law. Before the vote, the corporation shall give the director written notice of the grounds and a reasonable opportunity to respond. Cause includes serious breach of fiduciary duty, misuse of corporate funds, or persistent failure to perform board duties.

Directors receive no compensation for board service. The corporation may reimburse documented, reasonable expenses under its financial and conflict policies.

## Article 4 Board meetings and decisions

The board shall hold an annual meeting during the first quarter and at least one additional meeting each year. The initial organizational meeting is held following incorporation under applicable Michigan law. The president or any two directors may call a special meeting.

Except for a meeting subject to a longer statutory requirement, notice stating the date, time, location or remote connection, and proposed business shall be given to every director at least seven days before a meeting, personally, by mail, or by electronic transmission. The initial meeting shall receive the notice required by Michigan law. Notice of proposed bylaw amendments or director removal must describe that proposed action. Notice may be waived as permitted by law.

Directors may participate remotely if all participants can communicate with one another. Remote participation counts as presence. Directors may not vote by proxy.

A majority of the directors then in office constitutes a quorum. Unless a larger vote is required by law, the articles, or these bylaws, an action requires a majority of directors present at a meeting with a quorum. With five directors in office, three constitute a quorum. An ordinary action requires two affirmative votes when three directors are present, or three affirmative votes when four or five directors are present. A tie fails.

Action without a meeting requires the consent of every director then in office, in writing or by electronic transmission. The secretary shall preserve all consents with the minutes. Informal email exchanges or majority-only email approvals do not satisfy this requirement.

## Article 5 Officers

The officers are a president, secretary, and treasurer, elected by the board at its organizational meeting and thereafter at each annual meeting. Officers serve one-year terms and until successors are elected and qualified, unless they resign or are removed. The board may remove an officer with or without cause; removal from office does not itself remove that person as a director.

The president coordinates programs and administration, presides at board meetings, and signs agreements within authority granted by the board. The president's office does not confer additional board votes or authority to override board decisions.

The secretary maintains minutes, notices, consents, bylaws, and other corporate governance records, and certifies board actions when needed.

The treasurer maintains financial records, oversees receipts and payments, prepares reports for the board, and coordinates required financial filings.

One person may hold multiple offices. When an instrument must be executed, acknowledged, or verified by two or more officers, one person shall not act in more than one capacity on that instrument.

## Article 6 Finances and records

The fiscal year ends December 31. The board approves the annual budget, bank accounts, authorized signers, and spending authority. Corporate funds shall be held in the corporation's name and used for its purposes, subject to donor restrictions.

The treasurer shall report receipts, expenses, account balances, and known obligations at each regular board meeting. A director other than the person principally handling funds shall review monthly bank statements for months with activity. A person shall not approve their own reimbursement; another disinterested director must approve a documented reimbursement within the approved budget, and the board must approve an expenditure outside that budget.

The corporation shall preserve minutes, accounting records, filed articles, bylaws, and tax records and comply with applicable record inspection and public disclosure requirements.

## Article 7 Conflicts of interest

Directors and officers shall disclose actual or potential conflicts promptly and annually. A person with a financial or other material personal interest in a proposed transaction shall disclose relevant facts, answer factual questions as requested, and leave the deliberation and vote. The remaining directors shall follow the adopted conflict-of-interest policy and applicable law before authorizing the transaction. No interested person may approve their own compensation or a transaction for their own benefit.

## Article 8 Amendments and governing law

The board may amend these bylaws at a meeting for which the proposed amendment was included in the notice. Adoption requires an affirmative vote of at least a majority of all directors then in office. Amendments shall conform to the articles, Michigan law, and the corporation's exempt purposes.

If these bylaws conflict with mandatory law or the articles of incorporation, the law and articles control.
